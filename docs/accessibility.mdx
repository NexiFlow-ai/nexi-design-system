Accessibility is not a review at the end; it is a property of every component in the kit, verified every time a story
renders. The target is **WCAG 2.2 AA**.

## What every component guarantees

- **An accessible name.** Every interactive element has one — text, a label, or `aria-label` on an icon-only control.
  An icon is decoration (`aria-hidden`); the name is on the control.
- **Visible focus.** Keyboard focus is a 2 px ring on every control, in both themes, never removed.
- **The WAI-ARIA pattern.** Tabs, menus, switches, trees, comboboxes, dialogs and separators follow the authoring
  practices: roving tab index, arrow keys, Escape closes and returns focus, outside click closes.
- **Target size.** A control a finger or a pointer must hit is at least 24 × 24 px.
- **Contrast.** Text and the borders that carry meaning hold 4.5 : 1 against their surface in Light and Graphite; the
  data palette holds 3 : 1 against both.
- **Status is never colour alone.** A chip, a dot or a tint always comes with a label or an icon that says the same.
- **Motion can be turned off.** One shell transition and one progress pulse exist; `prefers-reduced-motion` stops
  both.
- **The state ladder.** Every screen renders loading, empty, error, denied, offline and not-found states through the
  kit, each announced to assistive technology; a spinner never covers stale content.

## How it is tested

Every story in the workshop is also a test: it renders, its interaction runs, and an accessibility engine checks the
result — a serious or critical finding fails the build. The *Accessibility* panel in the workshop shows the same
findings interactively for any story, in any theme or language.

## Where the rules live

The principle is [DS-0008 — Keyboard and screen reader first](./principles#ds-0008--keyboard-and-screen-reader-first);

The one shape of every Nexi dialogue outside an organization — the lobby (`/welcome`, DS-5016) and the request intake (`/request`, DS-5015) are the same surface with a different intent. Four things and nothing else: the header (the Nexi wordmark, DS-1061, and one line of context the host writes — *Acme Logistics · Invitation* · *Start with Nexi* · *Request access*; the state word is the page's `h1`, never drawn), the thread growing from the top exactly as NexChat draws it — Nexi's lines as NexChat's assistant message (`NexiTurn` = `.nexus-message-assistant`: prose, no name, no mark), the person's as NexChat's bubble, the same 28px rhythm, the foot (the kit's line composer, DS-2008, pinned at the bottom, and whatever the dialogue puts beside it: a receipt, a wave while a voice is in the room), and one polite live region. The host's `main` is `.nexus-convo-page` — the viewport: the header stays, the composer stays, the thread scrolls between them. Without a context the header is absent (a dialogue inside a page of the shell — Prepare an organization). No word above the thread, no toggle, no link, no decoration. Presentational: the dialogue owns the turns; the host owns the page. ** (b)** (owner 2026-10-24 20:11): the frame scrolls as NexChat's thread does (DS-5003) — the frame spans the viewport and the thread is the **one scrolling box**, so its scrollbar sits at the viewport's edge, never beside the words; the header, the composer and the words keep the reading column (`--nexus-composer-max-width`) centred by their own margins; the thread's tail passes *behind* the composer's band with the band's room beneath the last line, and a new turn scrolls the thread to its very end — nothing cramped against the composer, no inner scrollbar. The finish scene of DS-5016 takes the thread's place in the frame.

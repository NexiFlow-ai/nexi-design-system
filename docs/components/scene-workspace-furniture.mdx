What sits around the canvas on the Scene page (08) and is NexForge's on purpose. The header line: the title typed over in place (DS-7021; a scene born from the door asks for its name first), the view switch (Board · Diagram · 3D · Present — a view the scene cannot show is disabled with the reason), *Objects* · *Details* · `⋯`. On the canvas: the Board's tools on the left edge with their keys (`ElementPalette`), the colour key — the meanings this scene gave its hues — and the two bars that float over a selection: `SelectionBar` (colour · shape · text position · arrange · lock · more → meaning · duplicate · details · convert · delete) and `RelationBar` (colour · line · direction · kind · words · more), both composed of the kit's `ScenePop` (DS-4011). Under the canvas: the Objects list (`o`) as the kit's `DataTable` sharing the selection, and the conversion panel — the preview as a `DataTable`, *Create* the primary, an error as `Hint`. The dock: NexChat's one-line composer — dictation ≠ command. The Inspector: a door, never a wall — one object: `Field` for the name · `Select` for the meaning · `PropertyList` · the binding · convert; a selection: what applies to all; nothing selected: layers · versions · history; a viewer reads the same under a `Restriction`. `ThreeDView` and `PresentView` are the kit's `Scene3D` (DS-4010) and the camera track over the Board (`← → Space` step, `Esc` exits) with NexForge's labels. Not a kit duplicate: the kit has no scene vocabulary; every control is the kit's (`Field` · `Select` · `DataTable` · `ActionRow` · `LinkButton` · `IconButton` · `Inspector` · `Restriction` · `Hint`); every number a token (WP-9: 34 raw colours · 11 radii · 31 spacings · 9 type sizes · 4 layers/shadows · 4 `!important` · 3 cell and 5 input overrides → 0).
